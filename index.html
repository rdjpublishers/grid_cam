What is Grid Cam?

Grid Cam is a free photo grid maker that runs in your web browser. You choose a layout, then fill each tile with a photo, either taken live with your camera or imported from your gallery. When every tile is filled, you can apply a filter and download the finished mosaic as one image.

You can pick from three tile shapes. Square and rectangle tiles build a grid of rows and columns. The circle option splits one round photo into equal pie slices (2, 4, 6, or 8). There is no sign-up, no watermark, and no limit on how many grids you make.

How to use Grid Cam

  1. Choose a tile shape. Select Square, Rectangle, or Circle.
  2. Set the size. For square or rectangle, pick the number of rows and columns (1 to 6 each). For circle, choose how many slices you want. The live preview shows the layout and tile count as you change it.
  3. Decide on grid lines. Turn "Grid Lines" on if you want thin gaps between tiles. Leave it off for tiles that sit flush together.
  4. Press Start Capturing. You will see your empty grid and a counter showing how many tiles are filled.
  5. Tap a tile. Choose "Take Photo" to use your camera, or "Import from Gallery" to pick an existing picture. Your browser will ask for camera permission the first time.
  6. Position your photo. Drag to move it, and use pinch, scroll, or the sliders to zoom and rotate. Tap "Use Photo" to confirm.
  7. Fill the remaining tiles. You can tap any filled tile later to retake or replace it, or remove its photo.
  8. Pick a filter. Once all tiles are filled, a filter strip appears with looks such as Grayscale, Sepia, Vintage, and Noir.
  9. Download. Tap "Download Image" to save your mosaic as a PNG. Use "Edit Grid" to go back and change tiles, or "Start Over" to begin again.

Common use cases

  • Before-and-after collages. Show a workout, garden, room makeover, or drawing in progress across a two-tile or four-tile grid.
  • Photo stories. Tell a short story in six or eight frames, such as a recipe, a day out, or a trip.
  • Social media posts. Combine several moments into a single image instead of posting them one by one.
  • Product comparisons. Sellers can show one item from several angles, or several colors side by side.
  • Classroom and project work. Students can document a science experiment or craft project step by step.
  • Family and event keepsakes. Combine shots from a birthday, wedding, or reunion into one memento to share.
  • Pie-slice portraits. Use the circle layout for a fun profile picture, or give each slice a different expression.
  • Mood boards. Collect colors, textures, and objects on one canvas to plan a design, outfit, or decoration idea.

Tips for better results

  • Plan the layout first. A 2 × 2 grid suits comparisons, while larger grids work better for stories and events.
  • Keep lighting consistent. Photos shot in similar light look more cohesive side by side.
  • Frame with the tile shape in mind. A rectangle tile is portrait-shaped, so hold your subject vertically. In circle mode, keep the subject near the center of each slice.
  • Use the position screen. Zoom and rotate before confirming so faces and key details are not cropped awkwardly.
  • Choose a filter last. One filter applied to the whole grid makes mixed photos feel like a single set.
  • Consider grid lines. Gaps help when photos have similar backgrounds. Flush tiles look cleaner when the photos flow together.
  • Retake freely. Tapping a filled tile lets you swap a blurry shot without redoing anything else.

Troubleshooting

The camera does not start. Check that you allowed camera access when your browser asked. If you blocked it, open your browser's site settings, allow the camera for this page, then reload.

Another app is using the camera. Close video-call or camera apps, then try again.

The camera image looks dark or blurry. Move to better light and wipe the lens. You can also take the photo with your phone's normal camera and use "Import from Gallery" instead.

The photo is cropped wrongly. Use the Position & Scale screen to drag, zoom, or rotate before tapping "Use Photo." You can reopen the tile at any time to adjust it.

The filter strip is not showing. Filters appear only after every tile has a photo. Check the counter to see how many tiles remain.

The download does not start. Make sure your browser allows downloads from this site, then check your downloads folder or history.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Grid Cam really free? ＋

Yes. There is no cost, no account, no watermark, and no cap on how many grids you create.

Do I need to install an app? ＋

No. Grid Cam runs in a modern web browser on phones, tablets, and computers.

Can I use photos I already have? ＋

Yes. Each tile has an "Import from Gallery" option, so you can mix live shots with saved photos.

What is the difference between the tile shapes? ＋

Square and rectangle tiles create a grid of rows and columns. Circle creates one round image divided into equal pie slices.

Can I change a photo after placing it? ＋

Yes. Tap any filled tile to retake it, replace it from your gallery, or remove it.

How many tiles can I have? ＋

For square and rectangle layouts you choose 1 to 6 rows and 1 to 6 columns. Circle layouts offer 2, 4, 6, or 8 slices.

Will grid lines appear in my image? ＋

Only if you turn them on. Grid lines are off by default, so tiles sit flush together.

Does Grid Cam work without a camera? ＋

Yes. If your device has no camera, or you prefer not to grant access, import all your photos from your gallery instead.

Privacy note

Grid Cam processes everything inside your browser. Your camera stream, the photos you take or import, and the finished grid are not uploaded to any server. Once you close or reload the page, your work is gone unless you downloaded it, so save your image before leaving.
